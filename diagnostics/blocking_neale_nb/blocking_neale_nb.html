Blocking Diagnostic (Rich Neale)

The diagnostic evaluates the blocking frequency in a model by longitude and season as determined by the meridional gradient above a threshold value of daily 500mb height following D'Andrea et al 1998.

Blocking frequency for case {{CASENAME}} ({{FIRSTYR}}-{{LASTYR}}) is compared with the observational and model ensembles {{MDTF_BLOCKING_ENSEMBLES}}. All cases, observations and ensembles are combined in a line plot of blocking frequency vs longitude for season {{MDTF_BLOCKING_SEASON}}. The annual cycle of blocking frequency is shown separately for the case, the observations and the model ensembles in Hovmüller figures (longitude vs time of year).

The blocking frequency maps (polar stereographic, Northern Hemisphere) use a 2D blocking index, following Davini et al. 2012: the same meridional 500mb height gradient test is applied at every latitude between 35N and 75N, using the heights 15 degrees to the north and south, so blocking is located in latitude as well as longitude. Maps are shown for season {{MDTF_BLOCKING_SEASON}} for each case, observational product and model ensemble (ensemble mean).

The two indices are not directly comparable. The 1D index (line plot) tests the gradients around 60N only (dashed circle on the maps) and counts a longitude as blocked if any of three tests shifted by -3.75, 0 and +3.75 degrees passes, so at 60N it is higher than the 2D index. The highest 2D frequencies, e.g. near 180E, are at 65-75N, north of the latitudes the 1D index sees. This 2D index is a simplified version of Davini et al.: it uses the 1D thresholds and none of their extra conditions, so frequencies near the edges of the 35-75N band (especially 70-75N) are likely too high.
